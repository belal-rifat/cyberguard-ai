Technique: Phishing (MITRE ATT&CK T1566)

Definition:
Phishing is a social engineering technique where an attacker sends fraudulent messages designed to trick a person into revealing sensitive information (passwords, credit card numbers) or installing malware. Messages are often disguised as coming from a trusted source such as a bank, employer, or well-known service.

Common indicators:
- Urgent or threatening language ("your account will be suspended")
- Mismatched or spoofed sender email domains
- Suspicious links that do not match the displayed text
- Requests for credentials, OTP codes, or payment information
- Poor grammar or unusual formatting compared to legitimate communication
- Unexpected attachments (invoices, shipping notices, "documents")

Sub-techniques:
- Spearphishing Attachment (T1566.001): malicious file attached to the email
- Spearphishing Link (T1566.002): malicious link embedded in the email
- Spearphishing via Service (T1566.003): uses third-party services like social media or SMS

Why it is dangerous:
Phishing is one of the most common initial access vectors used by attackers because it targets human trust rather than technical vulnerabilities. A single successful phishing email can lead to credential theft, malware installation, or a full network compromise.

Recommended mitigation:
- Verify sender address carefully before clicking links or replying
- Do not enter credentials on pages reached via email links; navigate to the official site directly
- Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all important accounts
- Report suspicious emails to the organization's security team
- Use email filtering and anti-phishing tools
- Conduct regular security awareness training for employees
